Awareness Guide

This was a controlled phishing-awareness exercise. Here's what to take away from it.

✅
No real password was collected, transmitted, or stored.

Every step of this simulation was designed so that credential values never leave your browser.

Warning Signs You Encountered

  1. Unexpected credential request — you were asked to "verify" your account without initiating the request yourself.
  2. Urgency — the message implied a deadline and a consequence for inaction.
  3. Suspicious sender/domain — the sender address used an unfamiliar, non-official domain.
  4. Generic wording — the greeting and message did not reference you personally.
  5. Suspicious link — the "Verify Account" button led to an unfamiliar login page rather than a known, bookmarked site.
  6. Account verification request — legitimate services rarely ask you to "verify" your account via an emailed link.
  7. Emotional pressure — the framing nudged you to act quickly instead of pausing to verify.

How to Protect Yourself

🔍 Verify Sender Addresses

Check the actual email address, not just the display name, before trusting a message.

🔗 Inspect Links

Hover over links to preview the real destination before clicking.

🚫 Avoid Unexpected Login Links

Never log in through a link in an email — navigate to the site yourself instead.

⭐ Use Bookmarks

Access important services through saved bookmarks rather than emailed links.

🔐 Enable MFA

Multi-factor authentication limits the damage even if a password is exposed.

📢 Report Suspicious Emails

Reporting phishing attempts helps protect your whole organization.

☎️ Use Known Channels

Contact IT or support through verified numbers or portals, not links in the suspicious message.

⏳ Don't Let Urgency Rush You

Pause and verify independently before acting on urgent-sounding requests.
